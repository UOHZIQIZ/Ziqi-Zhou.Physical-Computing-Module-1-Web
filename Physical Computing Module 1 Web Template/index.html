Module 1

Activity Research

In Activity 1, I made my first attempt to use the Blink sample application for Arduino to see the full flow from code compilation to uploading to an onboard LED blink.
In Activity 2, I primarily explored how to connect an Arduino to ProtoPie Connect so that data from physical sensors could influence the digital interface. By collecting sensor data via the Arduino and then using ProtoPie Connect to transmit that data to the interactive prototype, I was able to achieve real-time interaction between physical inputs and the digital interface. This activity gave me a deeper understanding of how hardware, data, and UI/UX prototypes are connected and work together.
In this activity 3, I connected Arduino to TouchDesigner and used a potentiometer to control a circular visual effect in real time. I explored how sensor data can be transferred through serial communication and used to change digital visuals. I learned how to check Arduino data, connect basic TouchDesigner nodes, and adjust visual parameters. This helped me understand how physical inputs can create interactive visual experiences.

Research Activity

Consumer Trend Research

I researched future home experiences using TrendBible and explored interaction examples from TrendWatching. I focused on sensory experiences and intuitive physical interaction as starting points for my project.

Category 1 — Home Sensory Experiences
TrendBible’s 2027 forecast discusses sensory-rich interiors and the desire for calm. This inspired me to explore darkness, light and moving imagery as elements of an interactive experience.
Category 2 — Intuitive Home Interaction
“Embedded Flexibility” discusses adaptable spaces and intuitive technology. This encouraged me to explore how simple physical actions could influence a digital environment.

Personas Research

I used Pew Research Center findings to develop two provisional personas: a simplicity-oriented technology user and a privacy-conscious participant. These are research interpretations, not interviewed individuals.

Category 1 — Simplicity-oriented Technology User
This persona represents someone who does not necessarily value complex personalization. I want to investigate whether clear feedback and simple controls support their participation.
Category 2 — Privacy-conscious Participant
This persona represents someone concerned about personal data collection. This encouraged me to consider clear explanations of camera use and an alternative mouse-control mode.

HMI Research

I used the instructor’s How Might We resources to turn research insights into open-ended questions. The questions focus on participation, understandable feedback and choice.

Category 1 — Sensory Participation
· Potential problem spaces include understanding action–visual relationships and accommodating different interaction preferences.
· How might we make physical actions visibly influence a digital environment?
· How might we support participation at a comfortable pace?
Category 2 — Understandable Interaction
· Potential problem spaces include discovering the controls and understanding camera use.
· How might we make the interaction easy to discover?
· How might we offer informed choices about participation?

Project Path

I want participants to reveal hidden fish through shading and influence their movement through a mouse or hand gestures. The focus is artistic exploration and sensory participation.

Art Path

Working Approach
I would prefer to collaborate to exchange ideas and combine technical and visual skills. If a suitable partner is not available, I can develop the project individually.
Possible Direction 1 — Mouse Control
Covering the Arduino light sensor gradually reveals the fish. The mouse guides a leader fish, while the others follow and maintain spacing.
Possible Direction 2 — Hand Control
Camera-based hand tracking guides the leader fish, while mouse control remains available. Tracking compatibility and stability require testing.

Project 1 Concept

Project 1 Concept

ART Path — Signal → Visual (TouchDesigner)
Concept:
Light Beneath the Dark is a proposed interactive artwork combining Arduino and TouchDesigner. The scene begins as a dark ocean. Covering the light sensor gradually reveals glowing fish, while restoring light makes them slowly fade away.
Interaction:
I plan to offer mouse control and camera-based hand tracking. Participants guide a leader fish, while the others follow with spacing, delay and smooth turning.
Artistic Exploration:
The project explores darkness, hidden life and the relationship between physical actions and digital environments. Participants gradually discover how their presence influences the scene.
Inspiration:
Studio Roosegaarde’s GLOWING NATURE inspired my exploration of actions revealing luminous life. My project interprets this relationship through sensors and digital imagery.
Next Steps:
I will first develop mouse-controlled fish movement, then connect the light sensor and test hand tracking. Testing will focus on understandable controls, natural movement and clear responses to shading.
